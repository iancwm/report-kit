\begin{briefheader}{Operations strategy office \textperiodcentered\ Decision brief}{Consolidate the two regional dispatch platforms onto one stack by Q3 FY27}
Running two dispatch platforms costs Harborline \$25.8M a year and leaves the
South region 4.7 points short of the on-time dispatch target. A three-wave
consolidation onto the North platform cuts run cost by \$7.9M a year, pays
back in about 19 months, and gives both regions one operating model.
\briefmeta{Decision owner}{Chief Operating Officer}
\briefmeta{Decision needed by}{30 June 2027}
\briefmeta{Audience}{Executive committee}
\briefmeta{Classification}{Internal \textperiodcentered\ fictional}
\end{briefheader}

\section{Recommendation}

\begin{decisionpoint}{Approve a three-wave consolidation onto the North platform}
Approve \$12.4M of one-off spend, including \$1.5M contingency, to migrate
South dispatch onto the North platform in three waves between July 2027 and
March 2028, and retire the South platform once Wave~3 has run cleanly for one
full peak season.
\end{decisionpoint}

Approval commits the business to three things: a single dispatch operating
model across both regions, a frozen feature roadmap on the South platform
from July 2027, and a named migration lead reporting monthly to the executive
committee. It does not commit the business to a new vendor; the North
platform is already contracted through FY30.

\begin{exhibitgrid}[columns=3]

Run cost falls from \$25.8M to \$17.9M, or 30.6 percent.
\end{metric}}
\exhibitpane{\begin{metric}{19 months}{Payback on \$12.4M one-off spend}
Payback stays under two years unless savings fall by more than 21 percent.
\end{metric}}
\exhibitpane{\begin{metric}{+4.7 pts}{South on-time dispatch uplift}
From 91.8 percent today to the shared 96.5 percent target.
\end{metric}}
\end{exhibitgrid}

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Duplication, not scale, drives cost.} Licences, support staff
    and integrations are paid twice; the consolidated stack needs 69 percent
    of today's combined run cost, not half, because volume still grows.
  \item \textbf{The South platform is the service gap.} South re-routes take
    27 minutes against 18 in the North, and South needs 61 manual
    interventions per 1{,}000 loads against 42.
  \item \textbf{The North platform already carries the load.} It handled 58
    percent of FY26 volume with three severity-one incidents in twelve
    months, against seven on the smaller South platform.
\end{enumerate}

\section{Implication}

\begin{decisionpoint}{What approval changes for each region}
The North region absorbs South volume on a platform it already runs, so its
work is capacity planning rather than change. The South region carries the
change: new dispatch screens, retrained planners, and a six-week parallel
run per wave. The finance plan should book savings from Q2 FY28 onward, not
from the first migrated depot.
\end{decisionpoint}

Deferring the decision by a year costs roughly \$7.9M of avoidable run cost
and pushes the South service uplift past the FY28 peak season. Deferral is
reasonable only if the South platform's vendor offers a price cut larger than
that saving, which the current contract does not allow.

\section{Risks}

\begin{redflag}{Peak-season cut-over}
Wave~2 overlaps the start of the October peak. Mitigation: move the four
largest depots last within the wave and hold a two-week freeze before
Black Friday volumes.
\end{redflag}

\begin{redflag}{Planner adoption in the South region}
South planners have used the current screens for nine years. Mitigation:
fund super-users at every depot and keep manual-fix rates on the weekly
dashboard until they reach the North baseline.
\end{redflag}

\begin{assumption}{Savings depend on retiring the South platform}
The \$7.9M saving assumes the South contract ends in April 2028. Keeping it
as a fallback beyond one quarter removes roughly \$1.2M of the annual saving.
\end{assumption}
